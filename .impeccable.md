## Design Context
### Users
Ordinary residents using phones, tablets and desktops to control their own home without learning Home Assistant. One home per installation.
### Brand Personality
Calm, clear, approachable. SMARTi brand, as requested by the creator: https://www.smarti.no/.
### Aesthetic Direction
Charcoal, warm orange, soft neutral surfaces; original SMARTi logo and locally bundled Bai Jamjuree. Quiet Scandinavian layout, both dark and light themes. Creator-requested optional Fjord, Forest, Sand and Plum palettes complement the original SMARTi palette, independently of light/dark mode; preserve the original logo and semantic warning colors. Avoid technical dashboard clutter.
### Design Principles
- Make current state, stale data and pending actions unmistakable.
- Primary destinations: Hjem, Rom, Enheter, Automasjoner, Utvidelser, Oppdateringer, Innstillinger. Utvidelser (Extensions) is one admin destination containing Integrasjoner and Apper tabs; the Apper tab and its content are available only in advanced mode. Normal mode can show Utvidelser with integrations. Administrative permissions remain separate from display mode. Settings categories: Hjem og personer, Sikkerhetskopiering, Nettverk, System, Tema og utseende.
- Show only real capabilities; no decorative metrics or simulated live success.
- Large touch targets, keyboard focus, reduced motion and readable contrast.
- Keep secrets and privileged operations on the backend.
- Mobile dashboards and desktop/tablet dashboards have independent persisted layouts and card configurations. Changes on either surface must never update the other. Mobile editing uses compact controls and mobile previews.
- Mobile device cards, including Climate, reuse device artwork at a smaller size. Preserve configured images and use semantic icons when no suitable device artwork exists.

- Display Home Assistant platform branding as SMARTi with the bundled SMARTi logo throughout the app. Keep backend identifiers, integration domains, URLs and original saved configurations unchanged. Third-party brands remain their own.
